Rotator cuff and deltoid strengthening for NSEA swimmers. Swimmers make hundreds of thousands of arm strokes each season, and these small, stabilizing muscles keep the shoulder centered and healthy through all of them. Choose a program, watch the demo, and check off each exercise as you go.

**Before you start**

- Only do these when your shoulder is **pain-free** . If you have current shoulder pain or tendinitis, talk to your coach and an athletic trainer or physical therapist first.
- Move slowly and with control. Lowering the weight should be as controlled as lifting it.
- Stop if something pinches or hurts. Mild muscle fatigue is normal; sharp pain is not.

## How to train

Sets × reps 2 × 10

Build up as you can handle it.

Resistance Light

Light band or 1–3 lb dumbbell. Finish every rep with good form.

Tempo 2 up · 2 down

No swinging or jerking.

Adjust Band tension

Step farther from the anchor or choke up on the band to make it harder.

## Reading the clock

The band exercises use a **clock face** seen from above. You stand in the center facing **12:00** . Your right side is 3:00 and your left side is 9:00. Each exercise tells you where to lift your arm, with separate positions for the right (R) and left (L) arm.

Raise the arm to **shoulder height** . As you get tired you won't reach as high. That's fine.
