## What are "power points"?

Power points turn a swim time into a single number, so times in different events, strokes, distances, and age groups can be fairly compared. The system measures a swimmer's time against a set of benchmark standards — the faster the swim relative to that benchmark, the higher the points.

?

## Why not just compare times directly?

Because a 50m freestyle time and a 200m butterfly time aren't comparable, and neither are a 10-year-old's time and a 16-year-old's time in the same event. Points level the playing field so we can answer "which of my swims was actually my best relative to my age and event?" without doing that math by hand.

?

## Where do the points come from?

They're generated from national times databases, comparing a swimmer's time in an event against the wider pool of swimmers of the same age and sex who've swum that event. The benchmark tables are updated periodically — usually annually — to reflect current national performance standards.

?

## Do higher points always mean a faster time?

For the **same swimmer, same event, same age**, yes — dropping time always raises points. But points measure speed *relative to what's typical for that age and event* , not just raw speed. That's why a swimmer can drop time and see a smaller points jump than expected, or a bigger jump than expected, depending on how the field around them is performing.

?

## What are power points actually used for?

- **Tracking progress** — one number to watch trend upward across a season, even across different events.
- **Selecting squads or relay teams** — comparing swimmers across different strokes and distances on equal footing.
- **Meet qualification** — some competitions set a minimum points score, rather than a specific time, as the entry standard.
- **Club or regional rankings** — points-based tables let clubs rank an entire age group rather than event by event.

?

## My child's points went down even though their time improved slightly — why?

A couple of possibilities:

- The benchmark tables were updated, which shifts everyone's points.
- Your child moved into a new age group, which uses different benchmarks.
- The improvement was smaller than what's "expected" for that age band this season.

None of these mean the swim was bad — points reflect a *moving* standard, not a fixed one. ?

## Is a good points score more important than a good time?

Not really — they're two different lenses. Time is the concrete, real-world measure of performance, and what matters for most qualifying standards. Points are a comparison tool, useful for context and progress-tracking. Coaches care most about a swimmer's own times dropping and technique improving — points are a helpful summary, not the goal itself.

?

## What's a "good" points score?

There's no single answer — it depends on age, event, and level of competition. As a rough frame, many systems set 1000 points as roughly equivalent to a national-level benchmark performance, with youth club swimmers typically building up from a few hundred points as they develop. Ask your coach where your swimmer's current scores sit relative to club and regional norms — that context matters more than the number alone.

?

## Where can we check our swimmer's points?

Ask your coach — most clubs use a rankings/database tool where you can look up a swimmer by name and see their times and points across every event they've swum.
