Five mobility routines built for swimmers. Chest, shoulders, and hips come first because they set your streamline, your catch, and your kick. Ankles, wrists, and neck get their own short routine. The routines build on the NSEA Pre-Practice, Shoulder Care, and Foam Roller guides: those guides wake you up and build strength, and these routines open the range that strength then has to control.

Before you start
  • Move into a stretch until you feel a pull, then stop. Pinching, sharp pain, numbness, or tingling means back off or skip the move.
  • Many swimmers are already loose in the shoulders. If you are very flexible, do the active moves (circles, slides, reaches) and skip long passive holds at the end of your range. Mobility you can control beats mobility you only get into. GMB has a video on stability work for hypermobile joints.
  • Breaststrokers: keep knee twisting gentle. Open the hips and ankles first, and never force the knees.
  • This is for healthy swimmers. If something hurts in the water or keeps hurting after a week of easy work, talk to your coach and an athletic trainer or physical therapist.

Which routine do you need?

Try these checks on deck. They take about a minute and point you to the routine that matches what you find. The shoulder check is adapted from the overhead reach test in GMB's shoulder mobility article.

Check 1 · Streamline reach
Reach both arms overhead, biceps by your ears.

Does your lower back arch or your ribs flare? That points to tight lats and a stiff upper back.

→ Routines 3 and 2
Check 2 · Pinch at the top
Same reach, slowly.

A pinch at the top of the shoulder suggests the shoulder blade is not moving freely.

→ Routine 3
Check 3 · Upper back and chest
Stand tall against a wall.

If your head or upper back will not touch the wall without lifting your chin or arching your low back, the chest and upper back are tight.

→ Routine 2
Check 4 · Hips
Sit with soles of feet together.

If your knees stay high above the floor or you cannot sit up tall, the hips need work.

→ Routine 4
Check 5 · Ankles
Sit back on your heels with the tops of your feet flat.

If you cannot sit back, or it hurts the front of the ankle, you have limited toe-pointing range for kick. This is close to the seiza sitting position GMB teaches.

→ Routine 5
Check 6 · Dorsiflexion
Kneeling lunge, front foot flat, knee toward wall.

If the heel lifts before your knee reaches about a hand's width from the wall, the ankle is tight for breaststroke.

→ Routine 5

How much

Daily 5
Every day

Routine 1 on its own is enough on a busy day. It touches chest, shoulders, and hips.

Focus routines
3–4× a week

Pick Routine 2, 3, or 4 for what you found in the checks. Do them before dryland or after practice.

Moving reps
6–10 slow

About 2–3 seconds each way. Breathe out into the harder part of the move.

Holds
20–60 sec

20–30 seconds for younger swimmers. Up to 60 seconds for older swimmers who need it.

Splash & Tidepool
Daily 5, short

Keep it playful. Fewer reps, shorter holds, and a coach watching form.

Riptide & Breakers
Daily 5 + 1

Add one focus routine two or three times a week.

Surge & Senior
Daily 5 + 2–3

Rotate focus routines across the week and add Routine 5 after practice.

Where this came from. The routines start from NSEA's own Pre-Practice Mobility, Shoulder Care, and Foam Roller guides. Each card names its source. Many moves and the dosing (holds of 30–60 seconds for shoulder work, 15–30 seconds for the kneeling lunge, 5–10 contractions for targeted stretches, and the idea of practicing a new range with movement afterward) come from GMB Fitness articles on hip mobility, shoulder mobility, morning mobility, and stretching. Moves marked "Standard practice" are common coaching and physical therapy drills added to cover ankles, wrists, neck, and chest, where those articles are thin. GMB's free mobility routine and its seiza article were not available in full, so nothing here depends on them.

GMB YouTube channel. The GMB Fitness channel is also a source for exercise names and order. The cue wording on the cards is NSEA's own. GMB teaches active adults, so treat its videos as a coach reference and not as athlete homework.

Watch links and drawings. A "Watch" link opens a GMB video at the chapter named for that move, so you land on the right moment. A video can show a slightly different version than the written steps (the hip rotations, for example, are seated in the video), so follow the steps on the card. Cards with no matching video have an original line drawing instead. NSEA does not produce or endorse the videos.

Videos. The coach-reference links come from the Mobility WOD playlist (198 videos). The series is aimed at adult strength athletes, and some videos use bands, pain balls, or firm tissue work that is not right for youth swimmers. Use them to learn ideas as a coach. When a video differs from this page, follow the written steps and cues here. NSEA does not produce or endorse these videos.
