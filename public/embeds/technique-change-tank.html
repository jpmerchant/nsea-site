How the model works

The tank follows the standard physics of a well-mixed tank with water flowing in and overflowing out. The gap between the tank and the incoming water closes exponentially: fast at first, slower as it gets close. That matches what coaches see and what motor-learning research calls the power law of practice.

Tank size is the old technique's reps per week × 48 weeks a year × the square root of years. The square root reflects that old habits get deeper with time but not in a straight line.

Inflow is every rep of the skill each week. Focused reps count extra, up to 20 automatic reps each at full attention, because deliberate practice changes a pattern faster than reps on autopilot.

Leak and quality normally improve as the change takes hold: unfocused reps fall back to the old way less often, and new reps get cleaner. Turn on Pure physics to hold them fixed. Then a high leak or low quality makes the tank level off warm, and it may never reach the done line.

Meets add a burst of old-way reps under race stress. The burst shrinks as the new technique sets.

The numbers are illustrative. They show the shape of change and which settings matter most, not a promise for any one swimmer.
